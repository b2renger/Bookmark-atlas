Bookmark Atlas

Group by topic

Bookmarks that use related words in their title, summary and tags are grouped together. “Auto” tries several numbers of groups and keeps the one where groups are best separated. Each run is kept, so you can compare them.

Grid

The collection laid out on a grid (a self-organising map). Every bookmark sits in one cell, and neighbouring cells hold related bookmarks. A larger disc means more bookmarks, so the grid shows where your collection is dense and which themes border which. Each cell is named after its most telling tag.

Topic tree

Topics that are close are gathered into families, and large topics are split into smaller ones. Open a pearl to go inside it. Names can be changed and a topic can be moved to another family; nothing is recomputed when you do.

    Threads

    Every bookmark on one line, and an arc between two that resemble each other. Arcs above the line cross from one topic to another; arcs below stay inside a topic. In order of time, a tall arc ties two bookmarks far apart in time: an idea you came back to. Point at a bookmark to see its threads, click to keep them, then follow one.

    How topics connect

    Two bookmarks are linked when one is among the ten most similar to the other. A line joins two topics that share many such links, thicker when they share more. Click a topic to see what it is closest to.

    Bridges

    Bookmarks filed in one topic whose closest neighbours are mostly in another. They are the places where two of your interests meet.

          Share a snapshot

          A snapshot is one file holding bookmarks with their tags, the clusters, the map and your saved views, as they are now. Someone with Bookmark Atlas opens it with the menu Atlas > Open snapshot… and sees what you see, without touching their own collection. It does not contain your Notion token, your grooming history or the raw Notion records.

          Clean the collection

          Each script proposes changes. Preview shows them without writing anything; untick what you do not want, then apply. A backup is made before every run, and any run can be undone from the history below. The order shown is a good order to run them in.

          Look up publication dates

          Goes online and reads, on each bookmarked page that has no publication date, the date the page declares for itself. For a GitHub repository it takes the day the repository was created. Nothing is changed by the lookup: what it finds is then proposed by the script “Publication date read from the page” above, where you review and apply it. Hugging Face models, datasets and spaces give the day they were created. X posts are left out, since their date comes from the post ID.

          Check the links

          Goes online and asks each bookmarked page whether it still answers. Nothing is changed by the check: the script “Tag dead links” above then proposes a tag for the pages that are gone. A page that refuses or is slow to answer is left alone. X posts are not checked.

            4Import

            Where your data lives

            Everything is in this one folder: your bookmarks, your grooming scripts, the backups and the Notion token.

            To move to another computer, copy this folder there, then pick it with the menu Atlas > Choose data folder…. The same menu moves your data to another place on this computer, such as a synced drive. Because the folder holds your Notion token, do not hand it to someone else: to share bookmarks, use Export selection.

            Import a file

            Two kinds of file: a CSV export of your table from Notion (••• menu, Export, CSV), or a bundle exported from another Atlas (“Export selection” in Bookmarks). No token needed. Links already in the collection are not duplicated.
